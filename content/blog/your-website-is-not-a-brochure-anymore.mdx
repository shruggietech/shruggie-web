Your next customer may not “browse” your website first.

An AI agent might.

At Google I/O 2026, Google announced major updates to Search that move it further into AI-powered, agentic territory. Search is no longer just about typing a phrase and scrolling through blue links. Google is pushing Search toward richer AI answers, task-based assistance, and generative interfaces that can help users compare, explore, and take action directly from the search experience.

That matters a lot for small businesses.

Because if AI Search is answering questions, comparing businesses, summarizing options, and helping customers decide what to do next, your website can no longer behave like a static online brochure.

It needs to clearly explain what you do, who you help, where you work, why someone should trust you, and what action they should take next.

In other words, your website needs to be useful enough for humans and understandable enough for AI.

## Search is becoming more action-oriented

Google’s Search updates point toward a future where people may not visit five different websites before making a decision.

They might ask something like:

- “Who builds websites for small businesses near Knoxville?”
- “Find me a dance fitness class with online options.”
- “Compare local nonprofits that offer community support services.”
- “Help me choose a web developer for a service-based business.”

In the old version of search behavior, the customer might click several links, skim a few websites, open a contact page, and maybe submit a form.

In the newer version, AI Search may summarize the options first. It may highlight services, compare businesses, pull out reviews or trust signals, identify locations served, and suggest next steps.

Google has also talked about bringing more generative UI-style experiences into Search, meaning results may increasingly include custom layouts, interactive elements, and richer responses based on the user’s query.

That does not mean your website is irrelevant.

It means your website has to carry its weight.

## The old “online brochure” website is not enough

A lot of small business websites still follow the same basic formula:

Homepage.  
About page.  
Services page.  
Contact page.  
Maybe a blog from 2019 that has seen things.

That used to be “good enough” for many businesses. Someone heard about you, searched your name, clicked your site, and checked whether you looked legitimate.

But AI Search changes the expectations.

Now your website may need to answer very specific questions before a person ever clicks:

- What services do you offer?
- Who are those services for?
- What cities or areas do you serve?
- Do you work with businesses, individuals, nonprofits, families, contractors, medical offices, dance studios, or something else?
- What makes you credible?
- Do you have reviews, testimonials, examples, case studies, credentials, photos, FAQs, or clear process details?
- Can someone book, request a quote, call, email, or start the next step easily?

If your website does not answer those questions clearly, AI Search has less useful information to work with.

And when there is not enough clear information, your business may get summarized poorly, compared unfairly, or skipped altogether.

Rude? Yes.

Predictable? Also yes.

## AI Search needs clarity

AI does not magically understand your business just because you technically have a website.

It needs signals.

- Clear service pages are signals.
- FAQs are signals.
- Local information is a signal.
- Reviews and testimonials are signals.
- Case studies are signals.
- Consistent business names, addresses, service areas, and contact details are signals.
- Strong page headings are signals.
- Schema markup can be a signal.
- A Google Business Profile that matches your website is a signal.

The goal is not to “game the algorithm.” That is usually where businesses get weird and start publishing 38 nearly identical city pages with all the charm of a damp receipt.

The goal is to make your business easy to understand.

- For humans.
- For search engines.
- For AI systems that may be summarizing, comparing, and routing customer intent.

## Your service pages need to get specific

One of the biggest mistakes small businesses make is cramming everything they do onto one vague “Services” page.

Something like:

> We offer customized solutions to help your business grow.

Okay. Great. So does everyone else with a laptop and a Canva account.

A better service page explains:

- What the service is
- Who it is for
- What problem it solves
- What is included
- What the process looks like
- What someone should expect
- What the next step is

For example, instead of saying:

> We build websites.

A stronger version would be:

> We build modern websites for small businesses that need a clearer online presence, better lead capture, stronger local search signals, and a site they can actually maintain without wanting to pull their hair out.

That gives both people and AI more context.

Specificity wins.

## FAQs are becoming more important

A good FAQ page is not filler content.

It is one of the easiest ways to answer real customer questions in plain language.

Small businesses should consider adding FAQs to individual service pages, not just one giant catch-all FAQ page.

Good FAQ topics include:

- How much does this usually cost?
- How long does the process take?
- Who is this service best for?
- What do I need before getting started?
- Do you offer ongoing support?
- What areas do you serve?
- Can this be customized?
- What happens after I submit a request?

These are the kinds of questions customers already ask before they buy.

They are also the kinds of answers AI Search may look for when helping someone compare options.

If your competitor answers those questions and you do not, guess who looks more helpful?

Spoiler: not you.

## Local signals matter more than ever

For local businesses, AI Search makes local clarity even more important.

Your website should clearly communicate where you are located, what areas you serve, and what types of customers you work with.

That does not mean every business needs a clunky paragraph like:

> Serving Knoxville, Maryville, Oak Ridge, Clinton, Powell, Farragut, Karns, Halls, Sevierville, and surrounding areas.

Sometimes that is appropriate. Sometimes it reads like someone spilled a map onto the footer.

The key is to be accurate and natural.

Your local signals may include:

- A complete and accurate Google Business Profile
- Consistent name, address, and phone information
- Service-area language on relevant pages
- Local testimonials
- Location-specific examples or case studies
- Photos of real work, real spaces, or real team members
- References to local partnerships, events, or communities when relevant

AI Search is trying to understand fit. Local context helps it do that.

## Trust markers are not optional

If AI Search is comparing businesses, trust matters.

Your website should give people reasons to believe you are legitimate, capable, and safe to contact.

Trust markers can include:

- Testimonials
- Reviews
- Case studies
- Portfolio examples
- Before-and-after examples
- Certifications
- Awards
- Years of experience
- Client logos, where appropriate
- Clear policies
- Real photos
- A real About page
- A professional email address
- Secure website setup
- Clear contact information

You do not need to look like a giant corporation.

Actually, please do not. Small businesses trying to sound like Fortune 500 companies often end up sounding like a printer manual in a blazer.

You just need to look credible, clear, and alive.

## Your website still matters

There is a lot of dramatic commentary about AI replacing websites.

That is not the practical takeaway for small businesses.

The practical takeaway is this:

AI Search still needs sources. It still needs information. It still needs business details, service descriptions, trust signals, and local context.

Your website is one of the few places online where you control the message.

Social media posts disappear into the feed.

Third-party directories may be incomplete or outdated.

Reviews are valuable, but they only tell part of the story.

Your website should be the clearest source of truth about your business.

If AI Search is going to summarize you, give it something accurate and useful to summarize.

## What small businesses should fix first

You do not need to rebuild your entire website overnight.

Start with the basics.

- Make sure your homepage clearly says what you do, who you help, and where you work.
- Create separate pages for your most important services.
- Add useful FAQs to those service pages.
- Make your calls to action obvious.
- Add testimonials, reviews, examples, or case studies.
- Check that your Google Business Profile matches your website.
- Make sure your contact information is easy to find.
- Use plain, specific language instead of vague marketing fog.
- Add structured data where it makes sense.
- Improve page speed and mobile usability.

None of that is flashy.

But it is the kind of foundation that helps customers understand you, search engines understand you, and AI systems represent you more accurately.

## The bottom line

Google Search is becoming more agentic.

That means customers may increasingly rely on AI-powered search experiences to find, compare, and choose businesses.

For small businesses, the answer is not panic.

The answer is preparation.

Your website should no longer be treated like a digital brochure that quietly exists in case someone wants to “learn more.”

It should be structured, specific, useful, and trustworthy.

Because your next customer may not browse your site first.

An AI agent might.

And if that agent is going to explain your business to someone, you probably want it working with better material than:

> Welcome to our website.
